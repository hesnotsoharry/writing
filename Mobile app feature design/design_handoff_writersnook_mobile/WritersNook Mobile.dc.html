WritersNook on a phone
Turn 1 · three directions
The desktop is three panes and a segmented view switch. A phone gets one pane at a time, so the real question is what replaces that switch. Below are three answers to it, plus my read on which desktop features survive the trip.
Comes over cleanly
BinderAlready there. Gains reorder, status, new scene.
EditorFormat bar over keyboard, focus mode, typewriter.
Story BibleCharacters, places, lore, custom types. Lists and text fields — native fit.
CorkboardOne or two columns, long-press to drag. Reads better than desktop on a small screen.
OutlinerColumns collapse into a row + expandable meta. Labels and status stay tappable.
GoalsRings, streak, heat-map. Arguably better here than on desktop.
Quick captureThe strongest mobile-native case in the whole app.
SearchFind across manuscript, jump to hit.
SnapshotsBrowse, read, restore. Diff view is a scroll.
AI assistantChat is a phone-native form. Verbs run off a selection.
InspectorBecomes a bottom sheet: synopsis, status, labels, linked entities.
View-only, or stays on desktop
Brainstorm boardsPan/zoom viewer, tap a card to read it. Authoring a node graph with a thumb is a bad trade.
Relationship mapSame: view and tap through to the entity, don't re-lay-out edges.
Replace-allSearch yes, destructive bulk replace no.
Compile / exportShare a scene, yes. Full manuscript compile belongs where the filesystem is.
Label & type managerApply on mobile, define on desktop.
BYOK / endpointsKey entry stays desktop; mobile inherits the paired config.
Everything above assumes the pairing model you already have: desktop is the source of truth, mobile is a joined device.
1a
The Shelf
Bottom tab bar. The desktop's segmented switch becomes five persistent tabs — Write, Binder, Bible, Goals, More. Every view is one tap from every other view, and the tab you're on is always legible. Costs 56px of vertical space on the editor.

The Desk

The tide went out further than it had any right to, and the road showed itself: a pale seam of packed sand running toward the islands, there for six hours and then gone.

Aldis would not take the boat. He gave no reason worth the name, only stood at the top of the slip with his hands behind him and looked at the water the way a man looks at a debt.

So she walked. The sand held under her boots, wet and grainy and colder than she expected, and for a long while there was nothing to think about but the placing of one foot and then the other.

Behind her, Hallow Quay shrank to a line of grey teeth. The gulls stopped following at the half-mile mark, which she took as advice.

By the third hour the light had gone flat and the islands were no closer.

The Sleeve
The desktop's three panes, folded. The app opens straight into the scene you were writing; the binder slides in from the left edge, the inspector pulls up from the bottom, and a single pill in the header swaps the centre pane between Editor, Cork, Outline and Bible. Fewest taps for a returning writer, most to learn cold.
