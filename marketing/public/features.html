The page

A page that gets out of the way.

The editor is built on one idea: the sentence in front of you matters more than the toolbar. Formatting appears when you select text and leaves when you don't. The page is set in Literata, a typeface drawn for long reading, and you choose the measure, the line height, and the theme — including a soft dark study for late sessions.

The story bible

A memory for your world.

Characters, locations, items, factions, themes, and lore each get a proper page — appearance, voice, history, relationships. Every entry links itself to the scenes it appears in, and a side panel shows exactly who is present in the scene you're writing right now. Book three will not forget the colour of her eyes.

The relationship map

Every thread in the book, drawn.

Every relationship you note in the bible — kin, ally, rival, sworn enemy — becomes a labelled thread between entries. The whole web of the novel becomes something you can actually look at. This one is live: hover an entry to follow its ties.

05
Corkboard & outliner

See the whole arc at once.

Zoom out and your scenes become index cards — synopsis on the front, status colour at a glance. Move them into a new order to fix the pacing and the manuscript follows. Prefer rows to cards? The same structure flips into the outliner: a sortable table of every scene's status, synopsis, word count, and labels.

Goals & streaks

A quiet nudge, never a nag.

Set a daily word target and Writers Nook keeps a gentle count. Hit it and the ring closes. Miss a day and nothing scolds you — the streak simply waits. There are no badges, no notifications, and nothing that treats your novel like a fitness app.

Your files

It's a folder on your disk.

No account is needed to write a word. Your manuscript is plain files in an open format, in a folder you chose, that you can open with anything. Point the app at your Dropbox or OneDrive and it backs itself up for free. Nothing of your novel is stored on our servers — not now, not with sync later.
